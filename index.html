GateCheck, BirthCheck & The Identity Ladder (Levels 0–4)

Definition: GateCheck is a vendor-neutral operating architecture in which a hardware unit's identity — serial number, IMEI/EID, multi-unit kit (bundle) hierarchy, and serialized component birth set — is validated at the moment a contract manufacturer creates the shipment event, carried as a single lifecycle record through logistics, retail, RMA, non-conforming-unit (NCU) quarantine, and authorized repair, and reconciled via BirthCheck against field telemetry to detect counterfeit and re-assembled ("Franken") devices.

Cite as: Panda, S. (2026). GateCheck & BirthCheck: Factory-Gate Identity Integrity and Component Birth-Record Reconciliation Framework (v1.0). Open Specification. https://swostipanda.github.io/#gatecheck
JSON Schema (.json)

Five Architectural Components (GateCheck)

  1. Declaration: Brand owner publishes item master data to the manufacturer network: serialized flag, identifier type per item (Serial, IMEI, SGTIN), kit flag, and declared child count per kit.
  2. Factory-Gate Validation: Rule Set v1 (FG-01 to FG-08) evaluated when the shipment notice is created; non-conforming shipments are rejected before departure with machine-readable line reasons.
  3. Single Lifecycle Record: One append-only record opened per unit at shipment creation; tracks IN_TRANSIT, RECEIVED, ALLOCATED, SOLD, ACTIVATED, NCU_QUARANTINED, AUTHORIZED_REPAIR, and SCRAPPED.
  4. Component Genealogy (BirthCheck): Binds serialized sub-assemblies (SoC, MLB, display, battery, secure element) at birth and reconciles field/RMA observations against the birth tuple.
  5. Audit & DPP Evidence: Role-scoped exposure for warranty adjudication, carrier lock/unlock, customs/brand protection, and EU Digital Product Passport (ESPR) compliance.

Identity Ladder — Maturity Model (Levels 0–4)

  • Level 0 — Batch: Units tracked by lot/quantity only; serials, if captured, live in disconnected supplier spreadsheets.
  • Level 1 — Receipt-Checked: Serials/IMEIs validated only at 3PL warehouse receipt, carrier activation, or warranty claim — after defects have entered inventory and finance.
  • Level 2 — Gate-Validated (GateCheck Core): Identity validated at contract-manufacturer shipment creation against declared master data; multi-unit kits validated for parent–child completeness.
  • Level 3 — Lifecycle-Continuous: Single unit record carried from factory gate through retail, NCU quarantine, and authorized repair; warranty and carrier systems read one source of truth.
  • Level 4 — Genealogy-Verified (BirthCheck & DPP-Ready): Component-level birth records reconciled against field telemetry and repair scans; automated detection of "Franken-devices" and cloned component serials.

StoreNode Reference Architecture, TimeSplit & Launch Promising Playbook

Definition: In StoreNode, each physical retail store is modeled as a planned supply node of the enterprise order-promising engine: store allocations are published via standard replenishment-order constructs rather than bespoke mirrored-ATP syncs, and TimeSplit applies channel-segmentation rules across both on-hand and ASN-driven in-transit horizons so online orders, buy-online-pick-up-in-store (BOPIS), and store pre-orders are promised by one engine against one view of supply.

Cite as: Panda, S. (2026). StoreNode Reference Architecture and TimeSplit Time-Phased Channel Segmentation (v1.0). Open Specification. https://swostipanda.github.io/#storenode

StoreNode + TimeSplit Mechanics

  • Supply Publication via Standard Replenishment Constructs: Store allocations enter the enterprise promising engine as planned receipts/replenishment orders, removing the race conditions and double-selling caused by mirroring store shelf ATP into e-commerce during high-velocity launches.
  • Time-Phased Horizon Segmentation (TimeSplit): Channel segments (WALK_IN, ONLINE_BOPIS, REPLACEMENT_WARRANTY) exist per time bucket (On-Hand, ETA+0, ETA+1… driven by inbound ASNs). Store pre-orders consume the in-transit segment of the matching channel before the truck arrives.
  • Proportional Receipt Reconciliation: When physical received quantities differ from segmented ASN quantities, TimeSplit reconciles segments proportionally according to configurable channel priority rather than manual adjustment.
  • Walk-In Floor & Timed Spill Release: Enforces a minimum walk-in floor and per-store pre-order exposure cap; unconsumed pre-order segments release automatically into the pooled walk-in/BOPIS pool at a configurable T-minus hours before launch.

Launch Promising Playbook — 10-Step NPI Checklist

  1. Declare which channels may promise against in-transit supply (and which are restricted to on-hand).
  2. Publish store nodes and planned receipts to the enterprise promising engine 6 weeks before launch.
  3. Configure TimeSplit segmentation percentages per SKU family with an explicit walk-in floor.
  4. Set the maximum pre-order exposure cap per store and region.
  5. Bind inbound ASN ETA updates to promising horizon buckets.
  6. Dry-run launch contention with synthetic peak demand; eliminate race conditions in the sync layer.
  7. Configure receipt-discrepancy priority and automatic spill rules.
  8. Set the timed release window for unconsumed pre-order segments.
  9. Stand up store scrap and return-to-vendor inventory controls prior to launch day.
  10. Execute cross-functional Go/No-Go (Store Ops, E-Commerce, Supply Chain, Finance); freeze rules at T-72h and log every override.

Agent Delegation-of-Authority (A-DoA) & Autonomy Tiers T0–T3

Premise: Existing AI frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001) govern AI at the organizational level, while internal-control frameworks (COSO, SOX segregation of duties and approval matrices) govern humans and deterministic ERP workflows. A-DoA bridges the two by treating every autonomous AI agent as a formal delegate inside the organization's existing control system across four pillars: Delegate · Bound · Record · Own.

Cite as: Panda, S. (2026). Agent Delegation-of-Authority (A-DoA) Framework and Autonomy Tiers T0–T3 for Autonomous AI in Operations (v1.0). Open Specification. https://swostipanda.github.io/#adoa

Table 3A · A-DoA Agent Autonomy Tiers (T0–T3)

Tier Name Permitted Agent Authority Mandatory Control Evidence
T0 Observe & Recommend Ingest telemetry, correlate anomalies, and draft recommendations; human executes the transaction. Recommendation log + golden-set evaluation score
T1 Act & Notify Execute bounded, reversible operational actions; notify named human owner immediately post-action. Immutable A-DoA Decision Record + owner notification receipt
T2 Act Within Limits (Approve Above Threshold) Execute autonomously below monetary, volume, and frequency thresholds derived from the enterprise DoA; route above-threshold actions for human sign-off. A-DoA Decision Record + threshold evaluation proof + human approval link (when triggered)
T3 Act Alone With Audit Execute across the action class (e.g., automated safety/quality quarantine); subject to statistical audit sampling and strict runtime downgrade triggers. A-DoA Decision Record + quarterly control-owner sampling attestation

About the Author

Swosti Panda, SMIEEE

Swosti Panda designs the business systems that run physical-product supply chains. At Google Devices & Services she has authored the functional designs behind a sequence of launches since 2020: device-level serialization and identity integrity from the factory onward; the inbound-logistics and receipt services of an Oracle-to-SAP S/4HANA transformation; the omnichannel inventory architecture behind Google's physical stores; the hardware costing platform; and the product requirements for a multi-agent AI assistant for hardware failure analysis. Before Google she led BI and analytics for Sam's Club's retail fuel business at Walmart and configured SAP supply-chain and finance modules at Infosys Consulting.

She is an IEEE Senior Member, a Board Member of the IEEE Technology and Engineering Management Society (TEMS) Santa Clara Valley Chapter, an Observer on the INFORMS Franz Edelman Award Selection Committee (2026–2027), a peer reviewer for IEEE Access and IEEE Transactions on Engineering Management, and a contributor to ISM's Inside Supply Management and INFORMS Analytics.
